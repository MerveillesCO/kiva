# Merveilles Private-Club Pathway

## Working concept

Merveilles could be a selective cultural and social club centered on historical
craft, foodways, smoke and hearth cooking, seasonal gatherings, making, and the
Renaissance Faire community. Kiva would be one member program rather than the
club's sole reason to exist.

That is a credible concept. It is not, by itself, a zoning or food-license
exemption.

## Two structures with different consequences

| Structure | Owner economics | Regulatory value | Main weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| For-profit membership business | Can create profit and property value for the owner | Little evidence that membership changes food-service or event classification | May be treated as a reservation-only restaurant/event business |
| Colorado nonprofit corporation, potentially seeking IRC 501(c)(7) recognition | Earnings cannot inure to insiders; owner transactions need careful conflict controls and fair terms | May support a bona fide-club fact pattern and raises a possible Colorado nonprofit-event food exclusion | Governance, filings, private-benefit limits, and zoning still apply |

Colorado nonprofit incorporation and federal tax exemption are separate. A
Colorado nonprofit is not automatically exempt from federal income tax, state
tax, sales tax, zoning, or licensing. A social club seeking federal recognition
would generally apply under IRC 501(c)(7), not assume that status.

## What a genuine Merveilles club would look like

- a written cultural/social purpose broader than buying meals;
- limited individual membership with an application and meaningful acceptance;
- bylaws, a board, member rights, meetings, and financial records;
- annual dues that support the club, separate from event charges;
- a calendar including workshops, salons, craft demonstrations, volunteer days,
  member gatherings, and foodways education—not only dinners;
- events primarily for members and bona fide member guests;
- no public “join and immediately buy tonight's dinner” funnel;
- separate accounting for member dues, member event charges, nonmember income,
  property rent, food, and improvements; and
- no representation that membership overrides zoning, health, fire, building,
  liquor, tax, or accessibility rules.

The IRS recognizes dinner clubs as a possible social-club type, but requires the
club to be organized for pleasure, recreation, or similar nonprofitable purposes;
offer personal contact among a limited membership; receive support from dues and
assessments; and avoid private inurement. Public-facing and nonmember income is
limited and separately taxable. A club may have dining rooms, but that federal
tax treatment does not decide local land use.

## Property-value conflict

The current goal includes improving the value of Daniel's property. That is
compatible with an owner-operated for-profit project, but it creates a serious
conflict for a tax-exempt club if member money builds permanent improvements on
Daniel's land.

Before a nonprofit club pays rent, constructs Kiva, or funds permanent site work:

1. obtain independent nonprofit/tax advice;
2. use a written lease or facility agreement at supportable fair-market terms;
3. require approval by disinterested directors under a conflict-of-interest
   policy;
4. state ownership and end-of-lease treatment of improvements explicitly; and
5. document why the transaction primarily serves the club rather than the owner.

If private property appreciation and distributable operating profit remain the
primary objectives, do not force the project into a 501(c)(7) structure. Use a
for-profit entity and pursue the necessary land-use and food approvals honestly.

## Regulatory hypotheses to verify

### Planning

Ask El Paso County to classify a bona fide member-governed cultural/social club
with incidental member meals. Determine whether “club,” “private club,”
“membership organization,” “community facility,” “business event center,”
“eating/drinking establishment,” or another use applies and whether it is
available in RR-5. Ask whether frequency, dues, event charges, guest rules,
employees, advertising, or food becoming the principal activity changes the
classification.

### Public Health

Colorado's retail-food definition contains an exclusion for qualifying nonprofit
or charitable organizations serving food in conjunction with an event or
celebration in their home county, up to 52 days per calendar year. Obtain a written
determination on:

- whether a Colorado nonprofit social club qualifies;
- whether a recurring members' supper is an “event or celebration”;
- whether the exclusion covers preparation, service, or both;
- whether a private residential kitchen may be used;
- whether potentially hazardous foods or smoking processes change the answer;
- whether employees, paid chefs, event charges, or guests change the answer; and
- what sanitation, water, handwashing, and inspection authority remains.

Do not treat the 52-day language as approval until Public Health answers those
questions. It does not decide zoning.

### Alcohol

Do not assume a Colorado club liquor license is available. The statutory club
definition generally requires three years of incorporation, dues-paying
membership, and occupancy for specified nonprofit club purposes. Kiva should
remain alcohol-independent unless the state and local licensing authorities
identify a lawful route.

## Recommended sequence

1. Add the bona fide-club scenario to the Zoning Verification Letter.
2. Request a no-cost written/email issue-spotting response from Public Health
   specifically on the nonprofit-event exclusion before filing an RFE site review,
   using the [draft inquiry](public-health-club-inquiry.md).
3. Hold a founder workshop to decide whether Merveilles is genuinely intended to
   be member-governed and nonprofitable, independent of permitting advantages.
4. If yes, obtain Colorado nonprofit and federal tax advice before formation or
   property agreements.
5. If no, retain the club as a brand/community program only and model Kiva as a
   for-profit, reservation-only food/event use.

## Go/no-go gate

Proceed with the club regulatory hypothesis only if all four statements are true:

- Merveilles has a real purpose and program beyond Kiva dinners;
- the founders accept member governance and limits on private benefit;
- Planning confirms an attainable RR-5 land-use path in writing; and
- Public Health confirms the exact food pathway in writing.

Otherwise, membership may still be valuable for loyalty and demand, but it should
not be part of the permitting rationale.
