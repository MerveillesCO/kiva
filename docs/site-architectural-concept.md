# Kiva Site and Architectural Concept

## Concept status

This document establishes relationships and minimum design criteria. It is not a
survey, zoning exhibit, engineered site plan, fire-access plan, or permit drawing.
The parcel boundary, structures, utilities, grades, trees, easements, setbacks,
and distances must be field-verified before a location is fixed.

## Preferred site zone

Study Kiva immediately west of the house, beyond the existing open patio. The
sequence from east to west is **house → open patio → Kiva**, with Kiva opening
back toward the patio as its guest terrace. This owner-confirmed relationship
supersedes the earlier barn-adjacent concept. Service access must be designed
around the house/patio relationship rather than assuming direct barn adjacency.

The exact building envelope remains intentionally unfixed because the current
satellite and parcel graphics do not establish surveyed boundaries, well and
onsite-wastewater components, utility easements, contours, fire access, or mature
tree protection zones.

The owner identifies two proposed portable-toilet locations: one immediately
north of the barn and a second on the west side, screened just behind the garage.
The western unit is the likely closer Kiva sanitation option. Show both on the
survey and agency exhibits. Do not count either as satisfying Kiva's guest,
staff, handwashing, accessibility, or wastewater requirements until each
reviewing authority accepts the arrangement, measured route, service access,
screening, lighting, and maintenance plan.


Keep delivery, fuel, ash, refuse, and dirty-return movement on Kiva's outer west
or north service edge. Put the clean pass and guest-facing entrance toward the
existing patio and house. Guests arrive from the house/patio side on a signed
accessible route that does not cross the smoker yard, loading area, or dirty-
return path.

## Site design criteria

### Survey and legal constraints

- Confirm parcel lines and every recorded easement.
- Apply the controlling setbacks only after Planning confirms use and structure
  classification; RR-5 table values are not a substitute for that determination.
- Locate the well, septic tank, soil-treatment area, reserve field, and all utility
  lines. Protect required separations and future maintenance access.
- Map drainage, slopes, retaining needs, significant trees, and wildfire fuels.
- Verify whether any proposed surface or utility work triggers drainage, grading,
  driveway, or erosion-control review.

### Access and parking

- Preserve the existing driveway as the starting service and emergency route.
- Obtain fire-authority criteria for clear width, vertical clearance, grade,
  turning, surface, gates, address visibility, and water supply.
- Keep all guest and staff parking onsite. Do not count Capella Drive shoulder.
- Provide an accessible parking space and continuous firm, stable, slip-resistant
  route if required by the approved use and occupancy.
- Measure the actual route from Kiva and the patio to both portable toilets. Test
  the western unit behind the garage as the primary Kiva location and determine
  whether an accessible unit, handwash station, lighting, and screened waiting
  area are required.
- Separate guest arrival from delivery and hot-work movements with distance,
  barriers, scheduling, or all three.

### Neighbors and environment

- Aim smoker exhaust, equipment noise, task lighting, and service activity away
  from the house and patio, subject to actual wind, topography, trees, and fire
  review.
- Preserve a forested visual buffer toward Capella and Indi Drives.
- Use shielded downward lighting and an early operating curfew.
- Establish a shutdown threshold for wind, wildfire restriction, smoke drift, and
  unsafe weather before requesting approvals.
- Store refuse and cooled ash in animal-resistant, fire-approved containers.

## Building concept

Use a compact rectangular production core inside a low circular or faceted
guest-facing outer form. The simple core reduces construction waste, simplifies
washable finishes and equipment clearances, and supports conversion to a studio,
workshop, or garden pavilion. The outer porch, screen, and roof geometry can carry
the hearth character without forcing curved commercial-kitchen cabinetry.

### Preliminary 20-by-25-foot test fit

A 500-square-foot test rectangle sits at the midpoint of the current 400–600 sf
program. Final dimensions follow equipment cut sheets, required clearances,
plumbing, structure, accessibility, and occupancy.

Do not put a toilet inside the 500 sf test fit until occupancy, plumbing, and
Public Health requirements are known. If required, enlarge the building rather
than compressing food-safety clearances. A toilet serving guests must not open
directly into food preparation or storage space.

## Space and capacity tests

The [space program](../data/space-program.csv) assigns 500 sf to the enclosed
base case. The designer must test all selected equipment at true dimensions and
show door swings, drainboards, landing spaces, work aisles, accessible clearances,
and maintenance access.

The permanent financial case assumes 36 average covers per night. Do not size the
kitchen only for the ten-person pilot. Conversely, do not build for 36 covers
until Planning and fire authorities confirm that guest count, parking, occupancy,
water, wastewater, and neighborhood impacts can be approved.

## Material and systems palette

- Noncombustible concrete or masonry base at hot-work and service zones
- Simple conventional framed or mass-timber-compatible shell as approved
- Standing-seam or other ember-resistant roof with protected openings
- Lime/mineral-toned exterior, dark steel screens, and limited wood accents
- Smooth, durable, nonabsorbent interior food-zone surfaces
- Removable stainless equipment and non-load-bearing service partitions
- Daylight from protected high windows away from glare and pest entry
- Mechanical systems grouped on the service side and acoustically screened

The project should avoid an earth-covered roof, below-grade kitchen, site-built
unlisted hearth, or highly curved production shell in the first permanent phase.
Those features add waterproofing, structure, fire, sanitation, and resale risk
without proving the guest proposition.

## Drawings required before a building decision

1. Boundary/topographic survey with easements, house, patio, barn, utilities, trees,
   well, OWTS, and reserve field.
2. Code and entitlement diagram showing setbacks, use areas, parking, access,
   occupancy, and review-agency conditions.
3. Two footprints west of the patio plus a documented offsite/no-build case.
4. Equipment-based floor plan and reflected ceiling/lighting concept.
5. Exterior smoker yard plan with fuel, ash, clearances, barriers, and exhaust.
6. Water, wastewater/grease, electrical, fuel, mechanical, and fire narratives.
7. Adaptive-reuse plan showing the food equipment removed.
8. Schematic cost estimate tied to quantities and current local quotes.

## Site-selection gate

Select a permanent footprint only when one alternative demonstrates all of the
following: lawful use, surveyed setbacks/easements, well and wastewater
feasibility, fire access, required parking and accessible route, acceptable tree
loss and drainage, controlled smoke/noise/light impacts, utility cost within the
approved model, and a credible non-food fallback use.
