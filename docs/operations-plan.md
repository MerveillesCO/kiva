# Kiva Operations Plan

## Operating principle

Kiva is reservation-counted production, not speculative restaurant service. A
service proceeds only with a confirmed guest list, approved menu, adequate staff,
safe weather/fire conditions, working utilities and equipment, and completed
opening checks.

## Roles

| Role | Accountable for |
| --- | --- |
| Person in charge | License conditions, illness exclusions, logs, corrective actions, stop-service decision |
| Production lead | Receiving, raw-food control, cook process, temperatures, yield |
| Service/helper | Handwash readiness, protected service, allergen communication, dirty return, sanitation |
| Owner/host | Reservations, guest arrival, parking, boundaries, emergency communications, incident record |

One person may hold multiple roles only when the timed rehearsal demonstrates the
work can be completed safely. Public service requires the required Certified Food
Protection Manager coverage and trained food workers.

## Service cycle

### Seven days to 24 hours before

1. Freeze reservations and dietary disclosures by the published cutoff.
2. Issue batch sheet from paid covers plus approved buffer; no uncontrolled walk-ups.
3. Confirm commissary, suppliers, staff, weather, fire restrictions, potable water,
   wastewater capacity, fuel, and power.
4. Buy from approved sources and retain lot/invoice traceability.
5. Complete only commissary-approved advance prep; label and segregate food.
6. Cancel or move service if a critical dependency is unavailable.

### Load-in and opening

1. Record staff wellness; exclude symptomatic workers under the illness policy.
2. Verify site barrier, lighting, weather protection, fire lane, extinguishers,
   handwash, wastewater, refuse, pest controls, and guest route.
3. Calibrate probes and record receiving/transport temperatures.
4. Place raw food below and apart from ready-to-eat food.
5. Start equipment early enough to verify cold and hot holding before food enters.
6. Record an opening sign-off by the person in charge.

### Production and service

- Follow the approved batch recipe and log cook endpoints for each batch.
- Maintain cold food at 41°F or below and hot food at 135°F or above.
- Prevent bare-hand contact with ready-to-eat food.
- Use declared utensils and controls for each dietary alternative and allergen.
- Replace or wash/rinse/sanitize continuous-use utensils at least every four hours.
- Keep guests, animals, dust, smoke, raw-food work, refuse, and dirty wares out of
  the clean finishing and service zone.
- The person in charge stops sale of any food with uncertain source, time,
  temperature, contamination, or allergen status.

### Closing

1. Discard or lawfully cool food according to the approved process; no informal
   take-home reuse.
2. Seal and return dirty wares to the commissary, or complete approved warewashing.
3. Reconcile potable water supplied and wastewater captured; dispose at the
   approved location.
4. Cool and remove ash only under the approved fire procedure.
5. Complete temperature, sanitation, waste, sales, labor, yield, complaint, and
   incident records.
6. Correct deficiencies before the next service.

## Stop-service triggers

Stop or delay service for loss of potable water, handwashing, refrigeration, hot
holding, power needed for safety, wastewater containment, required weather
protection, safe smoke dispersion, fire authorization, required staffing, or an
uncontrolled contamination/allergen event. Call emergency services for fire,
serious injury, or medical emergency; preserve records and notify regulators or
insurers when required.

## Metrics after every service

Record paid and served covers, revenue, refunds, plate cost, waste, labor hours,
fuel/utility use, water/wastewater gallons, prep and service timing, temperature
exceptions, corrective actions, dietary requests, complaints, guest score, and
repeat/referral intent. Twelve compliant services—not twelve scheduled services—
are required for the permanent-building gate.

## Records retention

Keep approvals, menus, commissary agreements, supplier invoices, training,
equipment service, water tests, temperature/sanitizer logs, cleaning, incidents,
complaints, and corrective actions in a dated service folder. The final retention
schedule follows agency, tax, insurance, and counsel requirements.
