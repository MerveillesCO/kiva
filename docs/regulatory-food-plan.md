# Kiva Regulatory and Food-Safety Plan

## Controlling jurisdiction

Kiva is proposed at 21085 Capella Drive in unincorporated El Paso County.
AfterFaire references to Douglas County and Larkspur Fire do not apply to this
site. Primary authorities are El Paso County Planning and Community Development,
El Paso County Public Health, Pikes Peak Regional Building Department, and
Tri-Lakes Monument Fire Protection District.

## Recommended launch classification

Request a written site review from Public Health before investing in equipment.
Present three alternatives for determination:

1. licensed caterer prepares and controls all food;
2. Kiva operates as a temporary/special-event retail-food establishment with a
   licensed commissary; or
3. a permanent brick-and-mortar retail-food establishment after demand proof.

El Paso County states that home preparation of food and ice for special-event
service is prohibited. Extensive preparation must occur at the event's licensed
establishment or an approved licensed commissary. New fixed establishments
require plan review before construction.

## Launch menu boundary

### Included

- one smoked whole-muscle meat, cooked by an approved process;
- one smoked mushroom or vegetable main;
- pasteurized cheese served cold or smoked under an approved process;
- commercially produced bread;
- one hot or cold seasonal side;
- pickles/slaw and sauce from approved sources or approved commissary production;
- one shelf-stable or commissary-produced sweet; and
- water, coffee, tea, and approved nonalcoholic beverages.

### Deferred

- curing or smoking for preservation;
- vacuum sealing or other reduced-oxygen packaging;
- fermentation or acidification as a control step;
- canning;
- sous vide;
- raw or undercooked animal foods;
- cooling large intact roasts onsite without approved capacity;
- wild game or home-produced meat; and
- wholesale packaged products.

Deferred processes may require a variance, HACCP plan, process authority, or a
different license. Smoking is initially for flavor only.

## Process flow

1. Purchase from approved sources and retain invoices.
2. Receive under temperature control; reject damaged or out-of-temperature food.
3. Store raw animal foods below ready-to-eat foods.
4. Prepare at the licensed commissary unless the onsite permit expressly allows it.
5. Transport at 41°F or below, or 135°F or above, in clean protected carriers.
6. Smoke/cook using the approved recipe, equipment, endpoint, and monitoring log.
7. Serve immediately or hold hot at 135°F or above.
8. If cooling is approved, cool from 135°F to 70°F within two hours and to 41°F
   within four additional hours; otherwise discard leftovers.
9. Wash, rinse, sanitize, and air dry food-contact utensils at least every four
   hours during continuous use, or rotate clean utensils and return sealed dirty
   wares to the commissary if approved.
10. Capture all wastewater and dispose of it through the approved facility.

## Required operating documents

- approved menu and process list
- commissary agreement, if applicable
- equipment list and manufacturer cut sheets
- site and equipment layout
- water-source documentation and current test results
- wastewater and refuse plan
- employee illness and exclusion policy
- allergen matrix and consumer advisory, if required
- receiving, cooking, holding, cooling, reheating, sanitizer, and corrective-action logs
- cleaning schedule and pest-control plan
- Certified Food Protection Manager certificate
- fire approval and extinguisher inspection records
- traceability/recall contacts and incident report form

## Minimum temporary-service controls

- continuous hands-free warm running water, soap and paper towels at handwash
- dedicated wastewater container
- sanitizer and test strips
- overhead protection; sidewalls and floor where required
- food and clean utensils protected from guests, dust, animals, and weather
- calibrated 0–220°F probe thermometers
- cold holding at 41°F or below and hot holding at 135°F or above
- approved reheating equipment; holding devices are not used to reheat
- no liquid waste discharged to soil, street, or stormwater
