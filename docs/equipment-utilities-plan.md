# Kiva Equipment and Utilities Plan

## Procurement rule

Buy nothing fixed until Public Health accepts the menu, process, and equipment
layout and the building and fire authorities identify the applicable permits.
Every food-contact or temperature-control item must have a manufacturer, model,
listing, capacity, utility requirement, and cut sheet. Used equipment is purchased
only if its listing plate is legible and it can be inspected, cleaned, and serviced.

## Stage B — Seasonal pilot kit

The pilot kit is portable and reusable. Extensive prep occurs at the approved
commissary; the site performs only the processes Public Health approves.

| Function | Basis of design | Verification before purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cold transport/holding | Capacity for one service at 41°F or below, with backup ice or powered unit | Loaded temperature test for service duration |
| Hot transport/holding | Insulated carriers plus powered holding at 135°F or above | Recovery test after door openings |
| Smoking/cooking | Listed commercial outdoor appliance sized to the approved batch | Fire approval, listing, fuel, clearances, shutdown |
| Reheating | Commercial unit capable of rapid reheating; holding cabinet is not the reheater | Public Health acceptance and actual recovery time |
| Handwashing | Hands-free continuous warm water, soap, towels, wastewater capture | Water volume and service-duration calculation |
| Utensils | Four-hour changeout inventory or approved onsite wash/rinse/sanitize setup | Commissary return method and sanitizer test strips |
| Service | Washable covered tables, sneeze/guest protection as required | Approved site layout and weather plan |
| Monitoring | Two calibrated probe thermometers plus ambient thermometers and logs | Ice-point calibration before each event |
| Safety | Correct extinguishers, first aid, heat gloves, lighting, barriers | Fire authority inspection/acceptance |

Budget **$5,000–$15,000** including carriers, holding, handwash, work surfaces,
weather protection, smallwares, safety equipment, and contingency. Do not include
a residential refrigerator, home sink, crock pot as reheater, or unlisted homemade
smoker in the permitting basis.

## Stage C — Permanent equipment schedule

Final counts follow the menu and peak covers, not the room sketch. The minimum
program to price is:

- two-door reach-in refrigeration with separate raw and ready-to-eat zones;
- freezer only if the purchasing and menu plan demonstrates a need;
- listed outdoor smoker and secured fuel/ash area;
- rapid commercial reheating/cooking equipment and hot holding;
- dedicated hand sinks at locations approved by Public Health;
- food-preparation sink if produce or other food washing occurs onsite;
- three-compartment warewashing sink with drainboards, or an approved commercial
  dish machine plus required backup facilities;
- mop/service sink and protected chemical storage;
- stainless or other approved preparation tables and shelving;
- calibrated thermometers, sanitizer system, refuse, and pest controls; and
- ventilation, makeup air, suppression, and fire equipment dictated by the actual
  appliances and their listings.

The working equipment allowance is **$25,000 low / $55,000 base / $100,000 high**,
excluding utility extensions and building work. Obtain at least two quotes for
refrigeration, warewashing, hot equipment, and the smoker package.

## Utility design basis

### Water and hot water

Create a fixture-and-equipment demand schedule after the layout is accepted.
Confirm well legality, tested potable quality, peak flow, pressure, storage, and
treatment. Size the water heater from simultaneous handwashing and warewashing
demand plus recovery—not residential rules of thumb. Include backflow protection,
freeze protection, shutoffs, and accessible service clearances.

### Wastewater and grease

Measure Stage B water delivered and wastewater captured per cover. Use that data
to estimate permanent daily flow. Public Health must determine whether the
existing onsite wastewater system can serve the use, whether alteration is
required, and how grease must be intercepted and disposed. No kitchen or handwash
waste is discharged to the ground.

### Electrical and fuel

The electrical engineer receives a cut-sheet schedule showing voltage, phase,
amperage, connection type, heat rejection, and simultaneous-use assumptions.
Reserve capacity for cold holding, water heating, reheating, warewashing,
ventilation/makeup air, lighting, receptacles, and controls. The current PPRBD
code set includes the 2023 NEC and 2021 energy code. Fuel storage, gas piping,
solid fuel, ash handling, and emergency shutdown require fire/building approval.

### Ventilation and fire

Do not assume an outdoor smoker eliminates mechanical or fire requirements.
Submit the exact appliance, clearances, termination, roof/overhang relationship,
fuel storage, spark/ember control, grease production, and operating procedure to
PPRBD and Tri-Lakes Monument Fire. Preserve fire-apparatus access and keep guests
outside the hot-work perimeter.

## Utility cost gates

Before permanent design proceeds, obtain written feasibility and budget ranges
for: well/water treatment, wastewater and grease, electrical service, fuel,
ventilation/suppression, drainage, and accessible route. If base-case utility and
site work exceeds the current **$35,000 allowance**, rerun the entire capital and
property-value case before authorizing design development.
