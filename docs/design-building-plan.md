# Kiva Design and Building Plan

## Design intent

Kiva should feel like a small, elemental hearth pavilion rather than a suburban
restaurant: low, warm, round-edged, materially grounded, and oriented toward a
single shared table. Its operational core must nevertheless behave like a clean,
inspectable commercial food facility.

The architecture should separate five flows:

1. raw food receiving and cold storage;
2. smoker loading and hot work;
3. clean finishing, plating, and service;
4. dirty returns, warewashing, refuse, and wastewater; and
5. guests, who never cross production or service paths.

## Recommended site relationship

Locate Kiva in the existing barn/chateau service zone, not among campsites. Use
the existing driveway for deliveries and fire access. The smoker and refuse side
should face the service approach; the clean pass and guest table should face the
garden. Screen operations from Capella Drive and neighboring homes without
blocking fire access, ventilation, or sanitation service.

A surveyed site plan must confirm setbacks, easements, well and onsite wastewater
locations, drainage, fire access, parking, accessible route, and utility runs.

## Three-stage physical plan

### Stage A — Menu laboratory

Use a licensed commissary or licensed caterer for preparation. Test recipes,
yield, holding time, service choreography, cleanup, and willingness to pay with
private invited guests. Do not sell public meals during this stage unless the
specific food and event approvals are active.

Required portable equipment:

- NSF or otherwise approved hot and cold holding equipment
- calibrated probe thermometers and temperature logs
- insulated food transport carriers
- hands-free warm-water handwashing station
- wastewater capture
- smooth, cleanable prep/service surfaces
- overhead and side protection where required
- sanitizer, test strips, covered refuse, and pest protection

### Stage B — Permitted seasonal Kiva

Operate through the pathway approved by El Paso County Public Health: a licensed
caterer, temporary/special-event retail-food establishment, or another written
classification. Extensive preparation remains at the approved commissary. Onsite
work is limited to approved smoking, reheating, hot/cold holding, carving,
assembly, and service.

The temporary layout should test the eventual building geometry:

```text
SERVICE DRIVE
    |
[receiving] -- [cold/hot holding] -- [covered smoker yard]
                     |
              [clean finishing]
                     |
                [service pass]
                     |
GARDEN -------- [shared table]

[dirty return] --> [warewash or sealed commissary return]
```

### Stage C — Permanent Kiva

Proceed only after the demand and approval gates are met. The preliminary program
is a compact **400–600 square-foot food building** plus a code-compliant exterior
smoker yard and a separate guest terrace. This is a programming allowance, not a
permit drawing.

Suggested rooms and zones:

| Zone | Preliminary area | Core requirements |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Receiving and dry storage | 45–70 sf | Shelving, protected deliveries |
| Refrigerated storage | 35–60 sf | Reach-ins initially; floor drain only if approved |
| Clean prep and finishing | 90–130 sf | Prep sink, cleanable counters, hot holding |
| Warewashing | 70–100 sf | Three-compartment sink or approved machine, drainboards |
| Handwash and staff support | 25–45 sf | Dedicated hand sink, lockers, chemical separation |
| Mechanical and janitorial | 30–50 sf | Mop/service sink, water heating, panels |
| Circulation and wall allowance | 80–120 sf | Safe working clearances |
| **Building total** | **400–600 sf** | Final size follows approved equipment plan |

The smoker should be outdoors in a secured, noncombustible work yard unless the
fire and building authorities approve an indoor listed appliance and exhaust
system. This reduces—but does not automatically eliminate—hood, grease-duct,
makeup-air, suppression, clearance, and wildfire requirements.

## Architectural language

- Low circular or faceted guest-facing form recalling an earthen kiva
- Noncombustible base and smoker enclosure
- Mineral, lime-plaster, stone, dark steel, and wood accents outside hot-work zones
- Deep covered service opening facing the garden
- Downward, low-level, warm lighting
- No faux-historic claims or combustible decoration near cooking equipment
- Acoustic and visual screening of fans, compressors, deliveries, and refuse

The production room itself prioritizes washable, nonabsorbent, light-colored
surfaces, coved transitions, sealed penetrations, pest exclusion, and accessible
equipment clearances over theatrical finishes.

## Durable property value and adaptive reuse

Design the permanent shell so it remains useful if the smokehouse business ends.
Value-supporting features should include conventional permitted construction,
good daylight, an accessible bathroom if approved, durable utility service,
storage, conditioned workspace, a simple structural grid, and removable food
equipment. Avoid embedding the resale case in a single proprietary smoker,
oversized grease system, or highly specialized room arrangement.

The preferred fallback use is a high-quality studio, workshop, garden pavilion,
or residential entertaining space that is lawful in RR-5. It must not be shown or
valued as a rentable accessory dwelling: County guidance restricts compensated
occupancy of accessory living quarters. Planning must approve both the operating
use and any fallback use.

## Utilities

### Water

- Confirm whether the domestic well may legally and physically serve the food use
- Test the supply as required by Public Health
- Size hot water from the approved fixture and equipment schedule
- Prevent cross-connections and provide required backflow protection

### Wastewater

- Do not assume the residential OWTS can accept commercial kitchen flow or grease
- Obtain Public Health determination before design
- Evaluate an approved grease interceptor and OWTS alteration only after process
  flow, daily gallons, and soil-treatment capacity are known
- Stage B captures liquid waste for lawful disposal if required by its permit

### Power and fuel

- Prefer electric hot holding and refrigeration
- Size service for refrigeration, water heating, ventilation, lighting, and
  future resilience
- Locate propane or solid-fuel storage only as approved by fire and building staff
- Provide monitored shutdown procedures and no unattended overnight smoking

## Building approvals register

Before schematic design is frozen, obtain written determinations from:

- El Paso County Planning: use classification, site-plan process, parking, access,
  events, signs, and whether a permanent food facility is allowed in RR-5
- El Paso County Public Health: retail-food classification, plan review, well,
  wastewater, grease, menu/process, and commissary requirements
- Pikes Peak Regional Building: occupancy, construction type, accessibility,
  plumbing, mechanical exhaust, electrical, gas, and permit path
- Tri-Lakes Monument Fire: appliance listing, hood/suppression, solid fuel,
  clearances, fire access, extinguishers, wildfire restrictions, and occupant load

## Construction cost gates

These are early allowances, not bids:

| Scope | Low | Base | High |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Professional survey, architecture and engineering | $12,000 | $25,000 | $45,000 |
| Planning, building, health and fire fees | $3,000 | $8,000 | $18,000 |
| Site, utilities, drainage and access | $15,000 | $35,000 | $80,000 |
| 400–600 sf shell and interiors | $100,000 | $180,000 | $300,000 |
| Food equipment, refrigeration and warewashing | $25,000 | $55,000 | $100,000 |
| Smoker yard, exhaust/suppression and fire systems | $15,000 | $35,000 | $75,000 |
| Terrace, lighting, screening and furnishings | $8,000 | $20,000 | $45,000 |
| **Subtotal** | **$178,000** | **$358,000** | **$663,000** |

Carry at least 20 percent design/construction contingency at feasibility stage,
giving planning envelopes of roughly **$214,000 low, $430,000 base, and $796,000
high**. Adaptive reuse of a code-suitable existing building could be cheaper, but
utilities, accessibility, commercial kitchen finishes, exhaust, and wastewater
can erase much of that advantage.

## Permanent-building gate

Do not commission permit drawings until all are true:

- written confirmation that the use can be approved on the property;
- a workable water and wastewater path;
- at least 12 successful permitted seasonal services;
- at least 30 paid covers per operating night demonstrated or contracted;
- trailing contribution margin of at least 35 percent before fixed property cost;
- a credible annual Kiva revenue plan above $150,000; and
- an appraiser-supported estimate of contributory property value, with acceptable
  lender, insurer, tax-classification, and resale implications; and
- capital plan showing debt service or invested capital can be recovered within
  an agreed owner horizon.
