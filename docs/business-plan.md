# Kiva Business Plan

## Product

Kiva is a small, prepaid smokehouse supper built around one shared table and a
disciplined reservation count. It supplies the signature meal for AfterFaire but
must ultimately earn demand beyond eight festival nights before permanent
construction is justified.

Initial guest promise:

- one carved smoked meat or substantial smoked vegetable main;
- smoked cheese, bread, seasonal side, pickle/slaw, sauce, and sweet;
- water and a considered nonalcoholic beverage;
- visible hearth craft without exposing guests to production hazards; and
- published ingredients and major allergens.

## Customer and occasions

Primary launch customer: an adult Renaissance Festival visitor seeking a calm,
hosted conclusion to the day. Additional markets requiring separate validation
and lawful approval include private suppers, chef collaborations, workshops,
small celebrations, and licensed offsite catering.

## Launch model

- 6–10 prepaid guests
- one seating
- fixed menu with declared dietary alternative
- no walk-ups
- one production lead and one service/helper role
- licensed commissary or licensed caterer
- eight initial AfterFaire nights only after approvals

Reservation-only service and small guest count reduce operating impacts, but do
not convert food service into a lawful residential home occupation. The launch
model is contingent on Planning identifying a temporary or discretionary land-use
path and Public Health identifying the corresponding food-license path.

Merveilles may ultimately be a bona fide social and cultural club, but entity
form does not replace the commercial base case at this stage. Until written
agency and tax advice supports another structure, use the owner-operated
food/event economics in this plan. The [entity operating models](entity-operating-models.md)
separate the club, licensed-operator, and private-hosting alternatives and their
money flows.

## Corrected unit economics

The AfterFaire source contains both $18 and $24 food-cost assumptions. Kiva adopts
**$24 per confirmed cover** until recipe costing proves otherwise.

For stand-alone analysis, allocate $70 of each bundled ticket to the supper:

| Per cover | Base |
| --- | ---: |
| Allocated supper revenue | $70.00 |
| Food ingredients | ($24.00) |
| Service consumables and payment | ($6.00) |
| **Contribution before labor** | **$40.00** |

At ten guests, contribution before labor is $400. With food labor of $175 per
night and a $75 sanitation, fuel, and permit allocation, Kiva contributes about
**$150 per sold-out night** before property overhead, marketing, insurance, and
capital recovery.

## Eight-night pilot

| Case | Guests/night | Covers | Allocated revenue | Direct costs | Pilot contribution |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Minimum | 6 | 48 | $3,360 | $3,440 | ($80) |
| Base | 8 | 64 | $4,480 | $3,920 | $560 |
| Sellout | 10 | 80 | $5,600 | $4,400 | $1,200 |

Direct costs include $30 per cover for food, consumables, and payment plus $250
per operating night for food labor, sanitation/fuel, and permit allocation. The
pilot proves product and process; it does not finance a building.

## Property-value contribution

Kiva may create value beyond operating profit, but construction cost is not the
same as market value. El Paso County values residential property through the
market approach, based on how comparable buyers react to features and amenities.
Permits are also reviewed so new improvements can be added to the property
record. A specialized commercial kitchen may contribute less than it costs and
could change tax classification or narrow the future buyer pool.

Treat property value as a separately evidenced return:

```text
total return = operating cash flow + independently supported property-value gain
               - capital cost - financing cost - added taxes and insurance
```

For feasibility only, test value contribution at 20%, 40%, and 60% of permanent
project cost; do not book any of it as a forecast. At the $430,000 base capital
envelope, those tests imply $86,000, $172,000, or $258,000 of contributory value.
The remaining $344,000, $258,000, or $172,000 must still be justified by operating
cash flow or nonfinancial owner benefit.

Before permanent construction:

1. Commission an as-is appraisal.
2. Have a certified appraiser review plans and relevant rural residential sales.
3. Obtain a lender opinion on residential versus mixed-use treatment.
4. Obtain an insurance quote for the completed use.
5. Ask the Assessor how the permitted use and improvements are likely to be
   classified, without assuming a binding advance valuation.

The permanent building gate requires a supported value contribution, not a
percentage chosen by the project team.

## Scale economics

A base permanent Kiva capital envelope of approximately $430,000 cannot be
supported by eight ten-person nights. At a 35 percent contribution margin and a
ten-year simple capital-recovery target, Kiva needs at least **$123,000 annual
revenue merely to cover $43,000 of annual capital recovery**, before financing,
tax, depreciation, and broader property overhead. The design plan therefore sets
a $150,000 annual revenue gate before construction.

Illustrative mature case for testing—not a forecast:

| Driver | Assumption |
| --- | ---: |
| Operating nights | 40 |
| Average covers/night | 36 |
| Average food and experience revenue/cover | $105 |
| **Annual revenue** | **$151,200** |
| Target contribution margin | 35% |
| **Contribution before property overhead and capital** | **$52,920** |

That $52,920 figure excludes annual insurance, marketing, administration, and
other fixed operating costs. The interactive workbook currently assumes $25,000
of such costs in the permanent case, reducing operating contribution to $28,000
and margin to 18.5 percent. The earlier 35 percent target remains a construction
gate, not a result supported by the present assumptions.

At the base $430,000 capital case and a purely illustrative $172,000 appraised
value contribution, $258,000 remains to be justified. Recovering that amount over
ten years adds $25,800 per year to the operating requirement. At 40 nights,
$105 revenue per cover, and $45 variable cost per cover, the model requires about
35.1 covers per night to cover nightly costs, annual fixed costs, and that simple
capital-recovery target. This is close to the assumed 36-cover attendance and
leaves little room for misses, financing cost, taxes, or major repairs.

The same assumptions imply operating cost of about $85.56 per served cover. A
35 percent operating contribution margin therefore requires about **$132 per
cover before capital recovery**. If the $25,800 annual simple capital-recovery
target is treated as an additional required return, the corresponding 35 percent
price is about **$159 per cover**. These are mathematical thresholds, not evidence
that customers will pay them.

This scale exceeds the intimate launch model and may trigger materially different
land-use, parking, fire, sanitation, staffing, and neighbor requirements. It is a
financial threshold to investigate, not permission to operate.

## Pricing tests

Pilot tests should compare:

- $149 garden-supper bundle with approximately $70 allocated internally to food;
- $85 stand-alone smokehouse supper;
- $105 premium supper with a nonalcoholic pairing; and
- $125 chef-table collaboration.

Add $135, $150, and $160 concepts to research before permanent design. The first
pilot should still test a credible launch offer rather than charging a permanent-
building price for a temporary setup. See the [demand and pricing validation
plan](demand-pricing-plan.md).

Record conversion, refunds, dietary requests, plate cost, waste, labor minutes,
guest ratings, and repeat/referral intent. Do not use alcohol margin to rescue the
base case.

## Phased budget

### Phase 0 — Determination and recipe proof: $1,000–$3,000

- Public Health site review and agency consultations
- recipe trials, yield sheets, thermometers, logs, and guest research
- no public sales and no permanent construction

### Phase 1 — Licensed pilot: $5,000–$15,000

- permits, commissary/caterer agreement, insurance
- portable handwash, holding, transport, service, and weather protection
- eight approved services with refundable equipment choices

### Phase 2 — Repeatable seasonal operation: $15,000–$50,000

- durable approved equipment and storage
- expanded approved calendar and demand channels
- no permanent kitchen until building gate is met

### Phase 3 — Permanent Kiva: approximately $214,000–$796,000

- design, approvals, site utilities, building, equipment, smoker yard, terrace
- base planning envelope approximately $430,000

## Go/no-go milestones

| Gate | Proceed when | Stop or redesign when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regulatory | Written lawful path identifies land use, food license, fire, water and waste | Any necessary use is prohibited or approval cost defeats demand |
| Menu | Three rehearsals hit safety logs, service time, yield and guest score | Cooling, holding, allergy or staffing controls fail |
| Pilot demand | At least 70% of offered seats are deposit-backed at target price | Demand depends on deep discounts or camping legality |
| Pilot operations | 12 permitted services with no critical safety event | Repeated violations, complaints, or excessive founder labor |
| Seasonal scale | Credible path to 1,400+ annual covers and $150,000 revenue | Eight-night demand remains the only proven market |
| Building | All design-plan gates and funded contingency are met | Capital relies on optimistic alcohol, lodging, or unapproved events |
| Property value | Appraiser supports meaningful contributory value and lender/insurer accept the use | Specialized improvements show little resale value or impair financing/insurance |

## Immediate work

1. Obtain written Planning classification of paid reservation suppers in RR-5,
   including whether the use is an eating/drinking establishment, business event
   center, agritainment, temporary use, or another category.
2. Request the $75-or-less Public Health potential-site review only after Planning
   identifies at least one plausible land-use path.
3. Ask Tri-Lakes Fire for a pre-design discussion of outdoor solid-fuel smoking.
4. Price three licensed commissaries and two licensed caterer partnerships.
5. Convert the menu into recipe/yield/allergen/process cards.
6. Test $85, $105, and $125 willingness to pay with at least 30 qualified prospects.
7. Produce a surveyed utility and access base map before selecting a building site.
8. Replace the illustrative financial model with vendor quotes and agency fees.
9. Obtain an appraisal-based opinion of the proposed improvement's contributory
   value before schematic design becomes permit drawings.
