# Kiva Land-Use and Entitlement Plan

## Current conclusion

Kiva is not supportable as an ordinary residential home occupation. El Paso
County's Land Development Code expressly excludes restaurants, catering, and
other food service from that category. Because the property is understood to be
RR-5, no public meal, advertised event, or purpose-built commercial facility
should proceed until Planning gives a written classification and application
path for the whole operation.

This does not mean Kiva is impossible. It means the project must win the correct
discretionary or temporary land-use approval before food, building, and fire
approvals become worth pursuing.

## Classification facts to submit

Ask Planning to classify one complete fact pattern rather than isolated pieces:

- owner-occupied five-acre rural residential property at 21085 Capella Drive;
- fixed-price, prepaid, reservation-only smokehouse supper;
- initial maximum 10 guests at one shared table;
- proposed eight summer evenings, with exact dates and hours;
- no walk-ins, amplified entertainment, alcohol reliance, or customer retail;
- two operating workers initially;
- food prepared through an approved commissary/caterer pathway;
- listed outdoor smoker and temporary covered finishing/service area;
- onsite parking and existing driveway, subject to agency review;
- relationship, if any, to separately approved lodging/camping; and
- long-term possibility of a 400–600 sf adaptable food pavilion only after a
  proven seasonal operation.

Do not characterize the use as private merely because reservations are prepaid.
If the public can buy admission, regulators may treat it as commercial/public.

## Paths to ask Planning to evaluate

| Path | Potential advantage | Material limitation | Rank |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Temporary-use approval for a tightly bounded pilot | Tests demand with little permanent capital | Duration, frequency, and repeat eligibility may be narrow | 1 |
| Licensed caterer at an otherwise approved gathering | Outsources food control | Does not itself authorize the land use or event | 2 |
| Genuine agritainment with subordinate supper | Code recognizes examples such as chuck-wagon dinners | Requires bona fide agriculture; overnight stays excluded; RR-5 availability uncertain | 3 |
| Variance of use for an event center or classified food use | Could authorize recurring use | Public process, uncertain findings, site plan and mitigation likely | 4 |
| Rezone to a district allowing the use | Clearest long-term use table if supported | Cost, neighborhood/master-plan, tax, and lending effects | 5 |
| Offsite commercial Kiva | Separates food impacts from RR-5 | Loses onsite concept and adds logistics/capital | Fallback |

This is a decision agenda, not a representation that any path is available.
Planning's written response controls.

## Agritainment caution

County standards describe agritainment as agricultural events and activities and
allow supplemental uses such as chuck-wagon dinners. They also exclude business
event centers, recreation camps, and overnight accommodations from agritainment.
Use this path only if the property has a genuine, documented agricultural
operation to which the meal is subordinate—not nominal agriculture adopted to
evade the underlying use classification.

## Variance and event-center precedent

Published County staff reports concerning RR-5 event venues state that a
for-profit assembly venue was not a permitted principal use and required a
variance of use; one report also anticipated a site development plan covering
parking, landscaping, and lighting. Kiva is smaller and food-centered, so those
files are warning precedents rather than direct determinations.

If Planning points to a variance, request the current 2026 checklist, fee,
hearing sequence, neighbor-notice radius, review-agency list, expiration terms,
and required reports before paying for design. Ask which findings are most at
risk on this parcel and whether Early Assistance is required.

## Entitlement narrative

The strongest factual narrative is limited and measurable:

- one owner-hosted table, maximum 10 pilot guests;
- limited dates and early closing time;
- all guest parking onsite with no road-shoulder parking;
- no amplified sound and no general event rentals;
- smoke, light, refuse, and service screened toward the barn service zone;
- objective wind/fire cancellation rules;
- licensed food pathway and captured wastewater;
- neighbor contact and complaint-response procedure; and
- no permanent construction until agencies, operating evidence, and capital gates
  support it.

Do not promise restrictions that make the mature financial model impossible. If
approval is capped near 10 guests and eight nights, Kiva remains a brand amenity
and pilot—not a business capable of supporting a $430,000 build.

## Written questions for Planning

1. What use category applies to the complete ten-guest prepaid supper proposal?
2. Is that use allowed, temporary, special, or prohibited in RR-5?
3. Would using a licensed caterer change only food licensing, or also land use?
4. Can a temporary approval cover eight identified evenings and recur later?
5. Under what facts could supper qualify as supplemental agritainment here?
6. Does combining supper with lodging or camping change the use classification?
7. If variance or rezoning is required, what are the current fees, timeline,
   findings, notices, reports, and site-plan requirements?
8. What parking, access, traffic, light, noise, smoke, water, wastewater,
   drainage, wildfire, and fire materials are required?
9. Can a 400–600 sf pavilion retain a lawful residential accessory fallback use?
10. Which parts of the response are binding, and what secures reliance?

## Decision sequence

1. Obtain Planning's written classification at no or minimal cost.
2. Eliminate any path Planning says is unavailable in RR-5.
3. Obtain fee, timeline, and report estimates for viable paths before filing.
4. Confirm health, fire, building, well, and wastewater feasibility for the same
   fact pattern.
5. Compare temporary pilot, discretionary approval, rezoning, and offsite food on
   cost, probability, time, operating cap, and property effect.
6. File only when the likely approval supports the intended economics.

## Current procedural direction

On September 8, 2026, owner-reported guidance from County planner Lacey Dean was
that an Early Assistance meeting probably is not necessary and that the owner can
request a Zoning Verification Letter. Follow that direction unless the County
changes it in writing. EDARP describes the Zoning Verification Letter as the
project type for verifying zoning, state verifications, and allowed uses; its fee
is based on the hourly research fee.

Submit the [draft request](zoning-verification-letter-request.md) as a factual
request for classification. Do not ask the letter to approve an undefined future
restaurant. Ask it to state what is allowed, what is not, and which application
would be required for each clearly bounded scenario.

## Denver home-omakase comparison

The Westword article about Domī describes four invite-only guests and a suggested
minimum $100 donation at a Five Points townhouse. The article does not identify a
zoning permit, retail-food license, commissary, or inspection. A second article
places it at 2360 Curtis Street, a property reported as D-AS, a mixed-use downtown
zone—not a rural-residential district comparable to RR-5.

This example therefore shows a low-visibility operating tactic, not a verified
legal pathway. Calling payment a donation does not necessarily remove food or
land-use regulation. Denver says all businesses require a zoning use permit and
identifies eating/drinking establishments separately; Denver home-occupation
"Food Preparation" is framed around off-premises consumption. Kiva should ask El
Paso County whether a genuinely private, host-invited meal with no required
payment is outside its land-use regulation, and separately whether any suggested,
minimum, bundled, deposit, or required payment changes that answer.
