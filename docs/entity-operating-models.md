# Merveilles and Kiva Entity Operating Models

## Decision

Do not form an entity solely to influence permitting. First obtain Planning and
Public Health responses using the proposed facts. Then choose the structure that
matches the intended ownership, governance, and economics.

## Model A — Owner-operated commercial Kiva

Daniel or an owner-controlled LLC owns and operates Kiva. Customers may join a
marketing membership program, but membership is not presented as a regulatory
exemption.

**Best fit when:** private profit, property appreciation, control, and a future
saleable business are priorities.

**Regulatory assumption:** reservation-only meals remain paid food service and
require the land-use, health, building, fire, water, and wastewater approvals
identified by the agencies.

**Money flow:** customers pay the operating entity. The entity pays labor,
ingredients, permits, insurance, utilities, rent if applicable, and taxes. Daniel
funds or owns the permanent improvements.

## Model B — Bona fide Merveilles social club

A Colorado nonprofit membership corporation conducts a real social and cultural
program. Kiva suppers are traditional club activities for members and bona fide
member guests. The club may later seek federal recognition under IRC 501(c)(7).

**Best fit when:** the founders want member fellowship, governance, and a durable
cultural institution even if no restaurant is built.

**Regulatory hypothesis:** Planning must identify a club use available at the
property. Public Health must determine whether the nonprofit-event exclusion or
another food pathway applies. Neither result is assumed.

**Money flow:** members pay dues and event charges to the club. The club pays its
expenses. Net earnings cannot inure to Daniel or another insider if the club seeks
or maintains 501(c)(7) treatment. Member meal and refreshment revenue connected
to club purposes can be compatible with federal social-club status; public and
nontraditional income requires separate tracking and may be taxable.

## Model C — Club host plus licensed food operator

Merveilles is a bona fide club, while a separately licensed caterer or food
operator prepares and controls food for approved club events.

**Best fit when:** the club mission is genuine but Public Health requires licensed
food preparation and service.

**Regulatory assumption:** the licensed operator may solve food-control issues but
does not solve the property's zoning. Planning must still authorize the club,
events, attendance, parking, and other site impacts.

**Money flow:** use written, market-based agreements. The club should contract for
food service. Avoid routing member charges through an owner-controlled food
company without disinterested review and tax advice.

## Model D — Owner hosts noncommercial research dinners

Daniel occasionally hosts personally invited, nonpaying social guests. There is
no advertising, ticket, required or suggested donation, deposit, membership sale,
lodging bundle, sponsor consideration, or repeatable public path to a seat.

**Best fit when:** refining recipes and hospitality while written classifications
are pending.

**Regulatory boundary:** this is useful only if Planning and Public Health confirm
the fact pattern as ordinary residential hospitality. It cannot produce demand or
price evidence, and it must not drift into compensated service.

## Property arrangement if the club uses Daniel's land

The property and club should not be blurred. Before the club pays for use or
improvements, document:

- the exact licensed area and permitted activities;
- term, event schedule, rent, utilities, cleaning, damage, and insurance;
- ownership and removal of furniture, equipment, and improvements;
- responsibility for permits, taxes, accessibility, incidents, and restoration;
- fair-market support for payments;
- approval by directors who do not share Daniel's financial interest; and
- termination if required approvals are denied or the club loses status.

A tax-exempt club financing a permanent Kiva structure that becomes Daniel's
property is the highest-risk arrangement. Prefer owner-funded adaptive
improvements, club-owned removable equipment, and a short initial facility
agreement until counsel approves a longer structure.

## Membership design if Model B survives agency review

- Written application tied to the cultural and social purpose.
- Admission decision that is not automatic or simultaneous with event purchase.
- Annual dues distinct from event prices.
- Voting or other meaningful member rights under the bylaws.
- Member directory and records of admission, dues, attendance, and guests.
- A member host for each guest; record who paid and whether the member was
  reimbursed.
- Programs other than food, with opportunities for members to meet and
  participate.
- No general-public booking calendar disguised by a nominal membership fee.

IRS guidance treats a group of eight or fewer as a presumed member-and-guest group
when at least one participant is a member and the club receives payment directly
from the member or the member's employer. For larger groups, the guidance uses a
75-percent member threshold with member payment. These are federal recordkeeping
and tax guidelines, not Colorado land-use or food permissions.

## Recommended provisional choice

Keep Models A and C alive through the agency responses. Treat Model B as viable
only if Merveilles genuinely chooses member governance and the agencies identify
a benefit. Use Model D for unpaid development dinners only after the requested
written boundary confirmation.

Do not form the nonprofit, sell memberships, lease the property, or advertise
club suppers before the first three gates are complete:

1. issued Zoning Verification Letter;
2. written Public Health interpretation; and
3. independent nonprofit/tax advice on governance and property arrangements.
